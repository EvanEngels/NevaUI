# 💡 Threads

|             |            |
| ----------- | ---------- |
| **Stage**   | 💡 Spark   |
| **Created** | 2026-09-09 |

## The idea

Lines that connect related elements across a layout, and that **hang** — they are ropes,
not strokes. Point at one element and every thread touching it pulls taut; the rest stay
slack.

## Why this one

Relationships in an interface are normally shown by a separate diagram or by nothing at
all. A thread puts the relationship on the page itself, between the two things it relates,
and gives it a state: slack when nothing is happening, taut when one end matters.

It also gives the archived [Tether](../archive/tether.md) rope a purpose, which is the one
thing that experiment never had. The archive note said this is the direction worth trying.

## Open questions

- What happens when both ends are held and the gap is wider than the rope? A tether let
  the hand give; a thread has no hand.
- Does a hanging line read as a relationship, or as decoration draped over the page?
- How many threads before it is a bowl of spaghetti rather than a diagram?
- What does a thread say to someone who cannot see it?

## How this could fail

- The lines are pretty and tell the reader nothing they did not already know from the
  layout.
- A hanging curve crosses other content and makes the page harder to read, not easier.
